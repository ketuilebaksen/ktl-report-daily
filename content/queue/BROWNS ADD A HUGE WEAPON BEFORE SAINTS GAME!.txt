The answer key. On Wednesday the quarterback declined to answer. On Friday his coach answered every one of them, so here are the ten pairs, side by side.

First, the title. The weapon is real, it is just not huge.

The title is "Browns Add a Huge Weapon Before Saints Game!"

Cleveland did add a weapon this week, and we can tell you exactly who it is. The Browns signed tight end Carsen Ryan to the active roster off their own practice squad, and filled the vacated practice-squad spot with running back Hassan Haskins, who spent this season in New England and has appeared in forty-four career games.

Is that "huge"? No. But it is not nothing either, and here is the piece the video does not connect: Harold Fannin Junior is on the injury report with a groin injury. Fannin is the tight end who caught ten passes for one hundred fifty-four yards against Pittsburgh, Cleveland's most productive receiver that day by a wide margin. A team that promotes a tight end to the fifty-three in the week its starting tight end turns up limited is buying insurance, not a weapon.

And Monken said as much about Fannin in this very press conference: "No, he'll be fine. Just a little sore." Asked whether it could be long term: "I don't know that. I don't know yet. I just know it's something that's flared up."

So the headline is inflated and the real story is somewhere else entirely, which, as it happens, is sitting in the first eight words of the transcript.

The actual news, which is in the first line.

The transcript opens with a mangled fragment: "Sugar Sugar start at quarterback."

Clean it up and it is the announcement everybody has been waiting four days for:

Shedeur Sanders is going to start at quarterback.

It was made official on Friday, November sixth. Deshaun Watson remains in the concussion protocol and is listed as questionable; he missed Wednesday and Thursday and returned in a limited capacity on Friday.

And then came the most important exchange in the whole session, because a reporter asked the question directly:

Q: "Is the reason not to go with Deshaun just because he didn't practice as much this week?"
MONKEN: "Exactly."

One word. The reason is practice repetitions, not health, and not performance. Monken spelled out the limitation: "He's still not able to do any team reps. He can do individual, he can do some routes, been in meetings, but you're not able to do any of the team reps, which makes it difficult."

So Watson may yet clear. He is just not going to have practised with the team when he does.

And now the reason we built this article the way we did.

On Wednesday we published a piece grading the sixteen questions put to Shedeur Sanders, and the honest finding was that he declined to answer several of the best ones. "I guess we'll see." "I really don't have an answer for that." A smile instead of a scouting report.

Then on Friday Todd Monken sat down and answered them. All of them. Not in response to the same questions, and almost certainly without intending to, but line by line, the coach filled in the blanks the quarterback left.

So here at Ktl Report, this is the answer key. Wednesday's non-answer on the left, Friday's answer on the right, ten pairs. If you watched one presser and not the other, you got half a document.

Pair one. Has your development continued?

Wednesday, Sanders: "I guess we'll see. I guess we'll see."

Friday, Monken: "Where he's improved is his footwork, his drops, his mechanics, understanding where to go with the ball, sense of urgency, in terms of working through his progressions, protections there. There was so many things he needed to work on. And he's done that like any young player."

That is the answer, and it is a specific one. Five technical items and a sixth that is not technical at all, sense of urgency. A quarterback saying "we'll see" and a coach producing a six-item list is the cleanest illustration we have of why you watch both press conferences.

Pair two. What are you seeing from the Saints defense?

Wednesday, Sanders: the smile, and "I could have gave that a decent answer, but now I'm just going to smile whenever it's just like some questions."

Friday, Monken: "Getting to the line of scrimmage was going to be half the battle this week, obviously, going there." And on the venue: "Pittsburgh is a tough place to play. All these places are tough places to play. So we're going to have to do a great job of handling the noise and obviously playing on the road."

So the game-plan concern is not the Saints' coverages at all. It is the snap clock and the crowd, getting lined up, getting the call in, getting the ball snapped, indoors, with a quarterback making his first start of the season. Sanders would not say it. Monken said it twice.

Pair three. How do you establish your own tempo and rhythm?

Wednesday, Sanders: "Y'all asking questions I probably didn't even think about, to be honest. So I really don't have an answer for that."

Friday, Monken: "I liked his confidence, liked the way he operated, looked very comfortable in terms of getting our guys in and out of the huddle."

The answer to a question the quarterback had not considered turns out to be the thing his coach watched for all week. In and out of the huddle, tempo, described by the only person in the building whose opinion of it counts.

Pair four. Does this offensive system fit you?

Wednesday, Sanders: "We in like week seven and we talking about the offensive system fitting me. We could have figured that out in training camp."

Friday, Monken: "I do think there is positives to the amount of reps he got during the fall. I think obviously there was a two-month gap there, but I think there was enough there in terms of our system, how we do things. He's been on every trip, games. He hears every call that we make. So if a player is diligent like he's been, then you expect there to be some carryover."

And on why he had those reps at all: "That's why we opened up the competition and we split the reps, because of his ascent."

Sanders said the question's clock had run out. Monken explained why: the fit was settled in camp, exactly as Sanders said, and the only open variable is a two-month gap in team repetitions. Both men gave the same answer. One of them was just willing to show the working.

Pair five. The rookie receivers.

Wednesday, Sanders: "Tapping back into that memory bank, you know, having those reps... so I don't think we lost a step."

Friday, Monken: "I think he's got the arm talent you're looking for. I think he's got a nice touch. So again, that's another area that's under construction. Not only our guys doing a good job of getting down the field, but our ability to push it down the field."

This is the one pair where the coach is more cautious than the player. Sanders says the chemistry with KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston has not lost a step. Monken credits the arm and the touch, then files the deep passing game under "under construction", a phrase he uses twice in ten minutes.

At Ktl Report we think that is the honest version. The reps happened in July and August; the throws have to happen on Sunday.

Pair six. When did you find out?

Wednesday, Sanders: "I would say the same time y'all got the news."

Friday, Monken: "The week gone for Shedeur been great. Been a really good three days."

Three days. Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. That is the entire preparation window for a first start, and it is the number to hold in your head on Sunday afternoon when something goes wrong on a third down.

Pair seven. What changes this week?

Wednesday, Sanders: "Nothing changes."

Friday, Monken: "Oh yeah. No, I'm fired up. I'm fired up to see him play. I mean, who wouldn't be? Let's go... This is what he's wanted. He wants to be a starting quarterback in the NFL. Let's go. Let's let it rip. Let's see what we got. We're not holding anything back. I mean, we're going to try to get after it Sunday and see what we got him at quarterback and the rest of us."

Here the two men flatly disagree, and it is the most useful disagreement of the week. The quarterback says nothing changes. The coach says they are not holding anything back.

If you want one thing to watch for in the first quarter on Sunday, it is this: does the play-calling look like "nothing changes," or does it look like "we're not holding anything back"? Those are two different first drives.

Pair eight. Who this is actually about.

Wednesday, Sanders, three separate times, refusing to elevate himself above Watson, including turning down credit he was offered: "I don't really want to input what I have done because then I'll be taking a credit."

Friday, Monken, unprompted: "This isn't a Shedeur thing. At times we've coached well around our players, at times we've not. Times we've played well around our quarterbacks, at times not enough. You got to play collectively well together and help the quarterback out."

Player and coach arrived at the same sentence from opposite ends of the building. Neither wants Sunday framed as a referendum on one man. Both know it will be.

Pair nine. The question each of them refused.

Wednesday, Sanders, asked about hiring an agent four days before his first start: "What that got to do with this game? Nothing. I'm focused on this game."

Friday, Monken, asked about a teammate dispute: "No, I didn't even see that. I don't see that from our guys... I don't really know exactly what you're talking about."

Both men were handed a question about something other than football and both declined it. We graded Sanders' refusal an F on the question. Monken's is a different case, and it needs its own section, which is next.

Pair ten. The long game, which only one of them could answer.

Wednesday, Sanders: "Play the long game, you know, within everything." He used the phrase and did not explain it.

Friday, Monken, asked about a conversation the two of them had in his office back in August, explained where the phrase came from:

"I think you have to be honest with them. Everybody wants it now. We do. The player does. But you have to be honest with them and say: what is the long game for you? What is it that you want in an NFL career?... You want a second contract, right? You want to maximize whatever measurable skill set the good lord has given you that we help you to achieve... Okay. If you don't get your dream right now, if it doesn't happen now, what is our job as coaches, as parents, as mentors? Where is it that you want to be, and when? You can either go this way and say 'I didn't get my way now.' Or you can say: 'Yet. I'm not there yet.'"

That is where "play the long game" came from. A quarterback who lost a camp competition used his coach's phrase eleven weeks later, and the coach then supplied the conversation behind it. The word at the end of it is "yet."

Answer key complete. Ten pairs, ten filled blanks.

The Verse question, and why we are going to be careful here.

The one piece of this press conference that is not about the quarterback needs the facts laid out before anybody has an opinion, because the transcript garbles all of them.

What happened. On a point-after try in Pittsburgh, Steelers guard Mason McCormick swept the legs of a leaping Jared Verse and put him on the ground. McCormick was penalised for it.

What Verse said, on Wednesday, November fifth: "The lack of reaction by those around me upset me a lot." He said a similar thing happened to Derek Barnett on a later field goal, and that it "upsets me very deeply."

And what he also said, which is the half that keeps getting cut: he took responsibility for part of it. He said he "could have invited my teammates in on the conversation" about what he planned to do, that he should have been better in his pre-game talk about Pittsburgh and its fans, and that "I didn't do enough on my part." Before the game he had said the Browns planned on "making Pittsburgh ours."

The context. Cleveland was flagged nine times for eighty-five yards in that game, including three taunting and unsportsmanlike-conduct penalties. Monken's Monday message was "We weren't very poised. And we didn't keep our composure," and "our message will continue each week, and that is speak with your play."

Now Friday. Asked whether Verse had come to him about it, Monken said: "No, I didn't even see that. I don't see that from our guys. Our guys love playing with each other." Asked how he walks the line on letting players express themselves, he said he does not need to hold a meeting about it: "I'm fine. I don't have to have a conversation with our guys on that."

And he made a point of saying his Monday line was general: "That wasn't completely directed at anyone... It's how we practice every day, how we treat people, how we play the game. I mean, I think we would say that to all of our children, wouldn't we? It's not really what we say, it's what we do."

Our read, marked as a read rather than a fact. A head coach four days from a road start with a second-year quarterback chose not to turn a player's complaint into a team meeting. That is defensible and may well be right. It is also plainly a choice, and "I didn't even see that," about a story that was national by Thursday, is a line a coach says when he does not want to litigate it in public.

What we are not doing is calling this a fractured locker room. One player said he was upset, took part of the blame himself, and said he had already spoken to his teammates directly. That is a conversation, not a crisis, and the people selling it as a crisis were not in the room for it.

The number that changed on Friday, and nobody noticed.

Buried in the middle of the session, Monken said this:

"I think we're going to have seven offensive players that'll be in their first or second year starting and that's pretty cool. That's pretty cool to see and work on those guys' development."

We checked it, and it is a better line than it sounds, because the number used to be six.

As of September, Cleveland's starters from the last two draft classes were eight in total: Quinshon Judkins at running back, Harold Fannin Junior at tight end, Spencer Fano at left tackle, Austin Barber at right guard, KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston at receiver on offense, plus Mason Graham and Carson Schwesinger on defense.

Count the offense. Six.

The seventh is the quarterback. Monken's number only becomes seven when Shedeur Sanders starts, which is to say the sentence is not a stat, it is the announcement restated as arithmetic. On Sunday in New Orleans, seven of Cleveland's eleven offensive starters will be in their first or second NFL season.

Now put that next to the thing everyone will shout about on Sunday: the worst first quarter in the NFL, outscored sixty-five to thirteen. Seven young starters is both the explanation and the reason to be patient with it, and you are not allowed to use only the half you like.

And note who else Monken reached for: "Where would Austin Barber be if we weren't playing him? Where would Spencer Fano be if you're not playing?" His own rookie linemen, named in defence of playing a young quarterback. The argument is not about Sanders. It is about the whole roster.

Tomorrow, and then Tuesday.

Sunday, one o'clock Eastern, in the Superdome, on CBS. Cleveland is five and three and has not lost two in a row all season. New Orleans is two and five, is winless at home, and has the second-worst first quarter in the league behind Cleveland's worst.

Watson is questionable, not out. Whether he is available as the backup was still open on Friday: asked whether Watson would be the backup if he cleared, Monken said "Haven't decided that yet. We're still working through that, working through his ability to clear it."

Then Tuesday, November tenth, at four o'clock, the trade deadline.

And our own list, one day out.

We printed four criteria on Thursday, before kickoff, so nobody could move the standard afterwards. They stand unchanged:

One. One turnover or fewer. Two. Any first-quarter points at all. Three. Four sacks or fewer. Four. One real connection with a rookie receiver.

Monken's Friday answers bear on two of them. On the fourth, he confirmed the arm talent and the touch and then called the deep game "under construction", so treat a rookie connection as a genuine test, not a gimme. On the second, his "getting to the line of scrimmage was going to be half the battle" tells you where the first-quarter risk actually lives: not in the throws, in the operation.

We grade ourselves on Monday either way.

The one that matters, and it comes straight out of Pair Seven.

Sanders said "nothing changes." Monken said "we're not holding anything back." Which one do you want on the first drive in New Orleans?

A quarterback managing his first start carefully, taking the completions, protecting the ball, which satisfies our first criterion and might satisfy nobody's appetite. Or a staff that turns him loose, throws it down the field at the rookies, and accepts that the turnover column might move.

Pick one, then tell us what you would accept as the cost of it. Tomorrow at one o'clock we find out which one Cleveland actually chose.

The bottom line.

The weapon in the headline is a tight end promoted from the practice squad in the week Harold Fannin Junior appeared with a groin injury, and the actual news is in the first eight garbled words of the transcript: Shedeur Sanders is starting, made official on Friday, with Deshaun Watson questionable and unable to take a single team repetition all week. The reason, in Monken's own one-word answer to a reporter who asked it plainly, is "Exactly", practice reps. Not health, not performance, not a decision about anybody's future.

But the reason we built this as an answer key is the thing nobody is putting side by side. On Wednesday a quarterback said "I guess we'll see" and "I really don't have an answer for that," and smiled instead of describing a defense. On Friday his coach listed footwork, drops, mechanics, where to go with the ball, sense of urgency, progressions and protections; said the week's real challenge was "getting to the line of scrimmage" and "handling the noise"; praised how he got men "in and out of the huddle"; and explained that the fit was settled in camp, leaving a two-month gap in team reps as the only open variable. Ten questions, ten answers, two days apart, two different microphones.

And the two sentences that do not match are the ones to watch tomorrow. "Nothing changes," said the quarterback. "We're not holding anything back," said the coach. One of those is going to be true in the first fifteen minutes, the fifteen minutes Cleveland has been outscored sixty-five to thirteen in across eight games, worse than anybody else in football.

Seven of eleven offensive starters in their first or second year. Three days of practice. A loud building where New Orleans has not won.
