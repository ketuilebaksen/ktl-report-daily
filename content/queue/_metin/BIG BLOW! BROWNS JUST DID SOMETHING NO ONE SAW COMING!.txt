The football content nobody will clip.

Two answers in this press conference contain actual football, and both are better than the headline.

The goal-line menu. Asked who decides the play inside the five:

"throughout the week and before the game, we talk about certain things, but ultimately it's the coaching staff, you know, his call and what he feels is best in that situation. And he give us a couple plays where we can choose from. But certain plays that we scheme up, right where they call it, and we run it and they got the perfect look, and the last couple weeks has been working for us."

"He give us a couple plays where we can choose from."

That is a menu, and it is a real delegation of authority. At the goal line Monken is sending in options and letting the quarterback pick based on the look he gets. For a first-year head coach, handing a quarterback that much say inside the five is a statement of trust, and Watson is careful to put the credit back where it belongs, twice: "ultimately it's the coaching staff... his call."

The reporter also references two specific calls: a counter to a player the transcript renders as "KC," and a design that kept the ball with Watson himself. A quarterback keeper at the goal line is a call you only make if you trust a man's legs and his decision-making, which, for a player described as coming back from twenty-two months, is its own kind of vote of confidence.

And the bye-week list, which is the single most verifiable thing in the transcript:

"biggest thing, just reflect, see all the mistakes and what we can learn from, and then how can we get better. All the little things that offence wants to do some self-scouting for."

Now put that beside what Todd Monken said in his own bye-week press conference, separately, to different reporters.

Monken: internal scouting. Watson: self-scouting.

Monken, after the Houston win: "we have to figure something out about starting a hell of a lot faster." Watson: "how can we start faster?"

Three items, matched almost word for word, by two men answering different questions on different days. We at Ktl Report went through both transcripts line by line to be certain of it.

Asked three times about his future, Watson produced the most disciplined ninety seconds of his media career.

Question three, on being open to coming back: "of course I want to continue to build on the legacy that I'm building. And, again, ultimately it's up to the front office. It's up to Jimmy and Dee. It's up to AB. It's up to Coach Monken and the city. Count the parties he named: his agent, God, the front office, Jimmy and Dee Haslam, Andrew Berry, Todd Monken, and the city. Seven, and never himself.

And he is not dodging. He is describing the job accurately. A player under contract does not negotiate his own extension, an agent and a front office do, and the player's job is to make that conversation easy by playing well. Which is word for word what he said: "all I can do is continue to play good football, win games."

There is no figure, no offer, no deadline and no timeline anywhere in this transcript. Any channel that puts a number on this clip invented the number.
