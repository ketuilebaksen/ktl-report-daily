The drought ledger. Six things that have to be true in Pittsburgh, and the quarterback decision Monken actually made this week.

First, the title. The decision is real, and it is the least shocking one available.

The video is called "Todd Monken Shocking QB Decision Before Steelers Game." As always, we settle the headline in the first section here at Ktl Report, and this week the headline is half right, which is more than most.

There is a quarterback decision this week, it is finished, and the video never mentions it. Here it is, with the paperwork.

On Monday, October twenty-sixth, at four twenty-two in the afternoon, Cleveland activated Dillon Gabriel from injured reserve and waived tight end Carsen Ryan to open the roster spot. Gabriel's twenty-one-day practice window would have expired Tuesday; if it had lapsed he could not have played again in twenty twenty-six without a release and an injury settlement. Then Thursday's practice report settled the order for Sunday: Deshaun Watson starting, Shedeur Sanders the active backup, Dillon Gabriel listed as Q B three, and rookie Taylen Green expected to be a healthy scratch as Q B four. Four quarterbacks on the fifty-three, which is what Mary Kay Cabot reported the club always wanted: they "always preferred to keep all four quarterbacks on the roster, and found a way to do it."

So the decision is: keep everybody, change nothing at the top, and pay a tight end's roster spot for the privilege. That is the opposite of shocking. It is a head coach refusing to make the dramatic choice, and in a week where Cleveland is one win from something it has not done since nineteen eighty-eight, refusing drama is arguably the decision.

Now, the video itself is not a news piece. It is a pre-game hype piece, and a well-written one. It also contains one of the best verified statistics we have checked all season, and five or six claims that need fixing. So rather than pretend it is reporting, we are going to do what a hype video cannot: take its central object, the twenty-two-year drought in Pittsburgh, and turn it into a ledger of what actually has to be true on Sunday.

The one stat in this video that is better than its hype.

Credit first, because this one is real and it is remarkable.

The video claims that only three quarterbacks since the nineteen seventy merger have engineered a game-winning drive in each of their team's first five wins of a season. That checks out. The Browns' own director of football communications, Dan Murphy, posted it after the Titans game: Watson is "just the third Q B since the nineteen seventy merger to have a game-winning drive on each of his team's first five wins."

The other two, which the video names but does not date: Troy Aikman in nineteen ninety and Matthew Stafford in twenty sixteen. (It is Aikman, not "Aman.")

And the companion number, from Baltimore's own preview of this game: every Cleveland win this season has been by four points or less. Watson has led a fourth-quarter or game-winning drive in every one of them.

That is the whole identity of this team in two sentences, and it did not need a single adjective.

Now the object. What the two thousand three number actually is.

Cleveland's last regular-season win in Pittsburgh came in two thousand three, Week five of that season, which the video gets right. As of October twenty twenty-five the streak stood at twenty-two consecutive regular-season losses in that stadium. A win Sunday would also be Cleveland's first season sweep of the Steelers since nineteen eighty-eight.

The video adds that ten players on the current fifty-three-man roster had not been born the last time Cleveland won there. We could not verify that count against the roster, so we are not asserting the number, but the shape of the claim is sound. A win in two thousand three means anyone born after that October is twenty-two or younger, and plenty of this roster qualifies.

Here is why the number matters beyond sentiment. A twenty-two-year road losing streak is not bad luck. It is a long series of games decided by the same few things: pressure on the quarterback, the run game, field position, and discipline. So the honest way to preview this game is not to ask whether Cleveland has the grit. It is to ask: what specifically has to be different?

Six items. This is the ledger.

Condition one. Somebody has to play right tackle.

This is the top of the ledger and the video does not mention it once.

Tytus Howard, the starting right tackle, did not practice Wednesday or Thursday. He is in the concussion protocol after leaving the Titans game in the third quarter, and on Thursday he was the only Brown listed as a full non-participant. Elgton Jenkins (knee) was a Wednesday non-participant and upgraded to limited Thursday. And guard Teven Jenkins is on injured reserve, having been hurt in practice before Week one.

Put that against the defining number of this offense: Watson has been sacked twenty-five times, the most in the National Football League. The one game in which Cleveland allowed a single sack, Nashville, with Howard on the field until the third quarter, is the same game in which the offense produced four hundred ninety-one yards. When Dawand Jones came in for Howard, the pressures resumed.

Pittsburgh's pass rush is not Tennessee's. T.J. Watt and Cameron Heyward were both given Wednesday off for rest, not injury, which means they are being managed toward Sunday rather than away from it. Alex Highsmith was limited with a groin.

If this condition fails, nothing else on the ledger matters.

Condition two. Clean football, which the video is right to shout about.

The video's strongest argument is that Cleveland's biggest opponent is Cleveland. It cites sixty-three total penalties, second in the league behind Green Bay.

We could not verify that exact count and rank from a primary source, so treat the number as the video's and not ours. What we can verify is the shape of it: against Tennessee alone, Cleveland committed eight penalties for forty-eight yards, including a twelve-men-on-the-field flag that handed the Titans a first down. Left guard Zion Johnson was flagged three times in that game.

And Monken said it himself after the win, in the plainest terms: "we're going to have to figure out how to start faster, that's for damn sure."

In a stadium where you have lost twenty-two straight, the margin for self-inflicted damage is zero. The video's line, if the Browns stop hurting the Browns, is not a slogan. It is the condition.

Condition three. Stop the run, and there are two backs now.

The video names Jaylen Warren, and it is right to. It also stops one name short.

Warren is Pittsburgh's engine: in the Steelers' sixteen-ten win over New Orleans in Paris he ran eighteen times for one hundred one yards at five point six a carry, with three catches for thirty-six more, and he opened the game with a twenty-nine-yard run on his first carry. He has four games this season with one hundred twenty-five or more scrimmage yards, tied with Kenneth Walker for the most in the league. We could not verify the video's "over five hundred rushing yards and five and a half per carry" season figures from a primary source, so we are leaving those to the video.

The name it misses is Rico Dowdle, who had nine carries for forty-one yards and six catches for forty-five in that same game. Head coach Mike McCarthy has been explicit that this is a two-back offense: "This was the one two punch I was referring to back in training camp," and "anytime we can just line up and give you Jaylen and Rico all day, that's a good day for us."

Now the Cleveland side of the ledger, and it is the scary part. Through six games this defense had allowed one rushing touchdown. Then Tony Pollard, not "Tony Pard", ran twenty-five times for one hundred twenty-nine yards and scored three. Cleveland allows one hundred sixteen rushing yards a game, twentieth in the league, and both of its starting interior linemen, Mason Graham and Mike Hall Junior, were limited Thursday with ankle injuries.

Two healthy backs against a bruised interior. That is condition three and it is genuinely in doubt.

Condition four. Do not trail early.

Cleveland has trailed by fourteen in Nashville, by ten at halftime in Baltimore, and by ten-nothing and then fourteen-nothing in games Monken himself compared to each other after the Titans win: "felt a lot like last week I told the team, you know, ten nothing, then fourteen nothing."

They survived all of it. On the road in Pittsburgh, against a team on a three-game winning streak, with a crowd that has watched Cleveland lose there twenty-two times in a row, surviving a fourteen-point hole is a different proposition. Cleveland scores nineteen points a game, twenty-eighth in the league. You cannot spot a good team two scores and rely on an offense ranked twenty-eighth to retrieve them in a hostile building.

Condition five. Make Rodgers uncomfortable, and one correction.

Aaron Rodgers is forty-two, and the video is right that disruption is the whole assignment. In the October first meeting in Cleveland he threw for two hundred ninety-nine yards and three touchdowns, took five sacks, and threw two interceptions, the last of them to Grant Delpit to end the game. Against New Orleans he threw for two hundred twelve.

Now the correction, and it matters because it is about a specific player. The video says this would be the perfect stage for Jared Verse to register his "first career sack." That is wrong. Verse has career sacks. What is true, and it is the real story, is that he has not recorded a sack as a Brown, a drought of six games that one analysis called the longest of his young career. He was also a Wednesday non-participant with a knee and was upgraded to limited on Thursday.

And the fuller picture, which is kinder to Cleveland than the Verse narrative suggests: this defense had nineteen sacks through six games, second-most in the league at the time, with eleven different players recording at least one. Cleveland's pass rush is not waiting on one man. It is a rotation, and it is sixth in the league in sacks.

Also, for the record: the defensive coordinator building it is Mike Rutenberg, not "Rottenberg", hired by Monken this year to replace Jim Schwartz.

Condition six. Win another one score game, which is the one thing they have proven.

Every Cleveland win this season has been by four points or fewer. Five of them. All five featured a Watson fourth-quarter game-winning drive. Andre Szmyt has hit two walk-off field goals in eight days and a fifty-six-yarder in Pittsburgh's last visit to Cleveland.

This is the only condition on the ledger that Cleveland has already satisfied five times out of five. It is also the thinnest possible way to win a road game, and the video is right that the rest of the league should be nervous about giving this offense two minutes. We would just add the honest half: a team that needs the fourth quarter every week is a team with a very small margin, and Graziano at ESPN called the five-and-two start "a mirage" on exactly those grounds, pointing to Watson's twenty-seventh-place QBR and a negative scoring margin.

Both things are true. The record is real; the margin is thin. Sunday tests which one travels, and holding both of those sentences at once is most of what we do at Ktl Report.

The two injury reports, read honestly.

The video says Pittsburgh is "dealing with an infirmary ward of injuries that has completely gutted their depth," naming Michael Pittman Junior, Roman Wilson, Sebastian Joseph-Day and Derrick Harmon, with DK Metcalf shouldering the receiving load.

Here is what the Wednesday report actually shows. None of those four players appear on it, which can mean they are already on injured reserve rather than that the claim is false, but it does mean we could not confirm their status this week and will not assert it. What the report does list for Pittsburgh: Jalen Ramsey (wrist) and Brandin Echols (hamstring) did not practice, Ramsey later becoming a full participant; Doneiko Slaughter (hamstring) did not practice; Ben Skowronek (calf) and Alex Highsmith (groin) limited; and T.J. Watt, Cameron Heyward and Darnell Washington all given the day off for rest, not injury.

Three veteran rest days is not an infirmary ward. If anything, Cleveland's Thursday report is the longer one: Howard out, Verse, Jenkins, Graham, Hall, Harden, Delpit, Schwesinger and Thomas all limited, with Dylan Sampson the one clear upgrade to a full practice.

The video's own point, "our locker room is experiencing a miraculous healing phase", is half right. Judkins is back, Sampson is back and trending up, Graham is back. But "healing" and "limited on Thursday" are different words.

And the five words from the other locker room.

While Cleveland content talks about ghosts and legacy, the only man in this story whose job depends on Sunday said five words about it. Mike McCarthy, on not wanting to be swept in the season series: "They got us once."

That is the entire Pittsburgh game plan in a sentence. They lost at Cleveland on the first of October. They know exactly how. They have had four weeks and a bye-adjacent stretch of wins to fix it, and they are on a three-game streak coming home.

A hype video tells you Cleveland has the grit. The other locker room is telling you it has the tape.

What we could not verify.

We say this plainly every week, and this week the list is longer than usual because the script is rhetoric rather than reporting.

Sixty-three penalties, second in the league behind Green Bay. Plausible and consistent with what we saw against Tennessee, but we could not confirm the count or the rank from a primary source.

Jaylen Warren's "over five hundred rushing yards and five and a half per carry." His Paris game checks out exactly; the season totals we could not source.

Ten current players not yet born in two thousand three. Unverified against the roster.

Jerry Jeudy making "the play of his life" on the final drive in the October first win. What we can verify from that drive: Szmyt's fifty-six-yard field goal with about ten seconds left, Watson's twenty-four of thirty-three for two hundred sixty-eight with a two-yard touchdown pass to Fannin, and Delpit's closing interception. We found no sourcing for a Jeudy play on the winning drive.

KC Concepcion's "hundred-yard masterclass by halftime." He finished the Titans game with eight catches for one hundred twenty-nine. A hundred of them by halftime we could not confirm.

Cleveland sitting as the three seed. The Browns do lead the AFC North, and the seed follows from that, but we did not independently confirm the exact seeding line.

The Pittsburgh "Week three" or "Week four" question. The Thursday-night meeting in Cleveland was October first; NFL.com files it as Week four and one preview called it Week three. We will use the date, which nobody disputes.

What we are not carrying.

We are not carrying "shocking" for a decision whose content is "keep all four quarterbacks and start the same man." If you want the real headline, it is that Gabriel is on the fifty-three and listed third.

We are not carrying "the Steelers are gutted by injuries." Three of their best defenders were resting, not hurt.

We are not carrying a timeline on Tytus Howard. Concussion protocols do not have them.

We are not carrying the suggestion that anyone has put an asterisk on these five wins that needs defending. Beating the teams in front of you is the only method available.

And we are not carrying hype as analysis. The emotion in this script is legitimate, a fan base that has not won in that stadium since two thousand three is entitled to feel something. We just will not let the feeling stand in for the six conditions above.

Your turn.

Six conditions. One of them Cleveland has already met five times out of five. Four are genuinely in doubt and one, the right tackle, has not practiced since Sunday.

So rank them. Which single condition decides this game? We put the right tackle first, because the quarterback has been sacked twenty-five times and Pittsburgh rested its two best rushers on Wednesday specifically so they would be fresh. Tell us where we are wrong, and bring a number.

And the one for the Dog Pound: twenty-two straight regular-season losses in that stadium, and a sweep that would be the first since nineteen eighty-eight. If they win on Sunday, what is the first thing you want to say to the people who called this five-and-two start a mirage?

The bottom line.

The quarterback decision happened on Monday afternoon and it was the quiet one: activate Gabriel, waive a tight end, keep four passers, start Watson, list Gabriel third, scratch Green. Nobody was replaced and nobody was traded, which is its own kind of statement nine days from a deadline.

What is left is a football game, and the number that defines it is not five and two. It is two thousand three, the last time Cleveland walked out of Pittsburgh with a regular-season win, and a sweep that has not happened since nineteen eighty-eight.

Breaking that does not require grit, which this team has already demonstrated five times in four-point games. It requires a right tackle, a clean sheet of penalties, two gaps closed against Warren and Dowdle, a first quarter that is not spent trailing, and an uncomfortable afternoon for a forty-two-year-old quarterback who threw for two hundred ninety-nine against them a month ago and has had four weeks to think about it.

Mike McCarthy already said the only thing worth quoting this week: "They got us once." Sunday is about whether once was a fluke or a pattern.
