Four times Monken refused the story. Friday in Berea, the one word answer on Tytus Howard, and the shocking decision that was him admitting he hasn't made one.

First, the title. The decision he announced is that he has not made one.

The video is called "Todd Monken Announces Shocking Decision For Week eight." Here is the decision, in his own words, from the last question of the press conference.

A reporter asked what the plan is for Dillon Gabriel on Sunday. Monken: "I don't even know yet... I haven't even [thought] that far ahead yet... Shedeur is our backup quarterback. So Dillon will probably be, I don't know if he would be an active third, or, I don't know if I'm there yet. I don't know if I'm there yet. I really haven't even thought about it. I was trying today just trying to get through Friday."

And then, laughing: "I knew our backup was. I know we have four of them. We can choose one of one of the four, right, to be that. I know where the first two are."

That is the announcement. A head coach on the Friday before the biggest game of his season saying, cheerfully, that he knows who his first two quarterbacks are and has not yet decided who the third one is. It is not shocking. It is honest, and it is the first time all week anyone has told us the truth about that depth chart, including us.

And a correction to our own piece from yesterday.

We will take this one on the chin, because that is how it works here at Ktl Report.

Yesterday we wrote that Thursday's practice report "settled the order for Sunday: Watson starting, Shedeur Sanders the active backup, Dillon Gabriel listed as Q B three, and rookie Taylen Green expected to be a healthy scratch as Q B four."

The first two are confirmed by Monken himself: Watson starts, Sanders is the backup. The "Q B three" part was a beat writer's projection, not a coaching declaration, and we repeated it as settled. Monken said on Friday he has not decided and has not thought about it. We should have written "projected third" rather than "listed as Q B three." That is our error, it is corrected, and the honest version is: two spots are known, the third is open, and it will be sorted on Saturday with the inactives.

If you only ever hear us tell you what other people got wrong, you should not trust us. So there it is.

The one word answer that actually moved this game.

Now the real news, and it is buried twenty seconds into a garbled transcript.

A reporter asked whether there is still enough time for Tytus Howard, the starting right tackle who had not practiced all week in the concussion protocol, to clear and play. Monken's answer: "Yes."

One word. Here is why it is the most consequential thing he said.

Howard is now officially questionable on the final injury report, and the reporting around it says Monken believes he is on track to potentially clear the protocol by Sunday. Compare that to where this stood forty-eight hours ago: a full non-participant on Wednesday and Thursday, the only one on the report, with no timeline because concussion protocols do not have timelines.

Yesterday we put "somebody has to play right tackle" at the top of our ledger for this game, and it was not close. Watson has been sacked twenty-five times, the most in the National Football League. The one game in which Cleveland allowed a single sack was Nashville, with Howard on the field into the third quarter, and it is also the game in which the offense produced four hundred ninety-one yards.

Monken also answered the follow-up nobody noticed. Asked how Dawand Jones, the man who replaced Howard, and who the transcript renders as "Dewan", has looked all week: "Been fine. Been fine." And on why that matters: Jones spent the summer competing with Spencer Fano and taking first-team reps, and Monken's view is blunt: "there's no substitution for reps... all the reps that he got is paying off."

So Cleveland walks into Pittsburgh with its starting right tackle questionable and a backup who has been in that lineup before, in the Jets game, and has had a full week of work. That is a meaningfully better position than the one we described on Thursday.

And the other return, which is the one for the run defense.

Mike Hall Junior has no injury designation and will play.

Monken on him: "He tried to go last week, just couldn't. A lot like Mason tried the week before that. So really excited to have him back in the rotation.", Mason being Mason Graham, who followed the same path back.

And asked directly whether Hall helps against the run after Tennessee ran for one hundred forty-one yards and Tony Pollard scored three touchdowns: "Oh, for sure. I mean, there's a number of reasons why they had the performance they did against us. Couple of misfits, ball got out on us a couple of times. But we certainly can be a lot cleaner there with our fits, and Mike helps with that."

"Fits" is gap-fit, the assignment of which defender has which gap on a run play. Monken is saying the three touchdowns were an assignment problem, not a talent problem, and that the man who fixes assignments inside is back.

There is also a lovely detail he volunteered about Hall's last appearance, in the October first win over Pittsburgh. Asked about Hall's form in that game, in which he recorded a sack, Monken went somewhere else: "what I remember most is him coming back, you know, from being knocked out and then coming back wanting to be with his teammates and finishing the game. It says a lot that he came back to play when you consider he's been out since then. I think that says a lot more about him and how he's wired."

And the roster mechanic hidden in one sentence.

One line from that same answer is worth unpacking because almost nobody will: "And Sam did a great job in the three weeks we elevated him."

That is Sam Kamara, a defensive tackle on Cleveland's practice squad, elevated for games including Week six against Baltimore. And "three weeks" is the whole point: under the standard practice-squad rules a player can be elevated to the active roster a maximum of three times per season. After that, if you want him on the field again, you have to sign him to the fifty-three-man roster.

So Monken thanking Sam Kamara for three weeks of work is also, quietly, Monken telling you that door is now closed, and that getting Mike Hall Junior back is not just a boost, it is the replacement for a option Cleveland no longer has.

Refusal one. It wasn't one player who got better.

Here is where this press conference gets genuinely interesting. Four times Monken was handed a ready-made storyline and four times he declined it. Each refusal is checkable, so let us rule on each.

The story offered: why was the protection so much better against Tennessee? Somebody must have played better. "I think Deshaun was only sacked one time."

Monken's refusal: "It's never just one thing. Protection was better. We got the ball out better. We're able to run the ball better. We're ahead of the chains. We schemed guys open a little bit easier than we did the week before, where he was able to, he ended up holding it. Some was him, some was us." And then the sentence that matters: "I don't think it was any different in terms of the way our players played... It wasn't like, 'Wow, this player played significantly better.' I just think we did things a lot better collectively offensively."

Our ruling: he is right, and the numbers support him over the narrative. Cleveland did not just allow one sack, it ran for one hundred eighty-three yards on twenty-nine carries, converted five of eleven third downs against a season rate that sat thirtieth, and produced twenty-six first downs. A line that suddenly blocks better in isolation does not also produce a season-high rushing total and a third-down jump. Those move together or not at all. Monken is describing a sequencing effect, stay ahead of the chains, throw on second-and-four instead of third-and-nine, and that is what the box score shows.

Refusal two. The Paris travel excuse.

The story offered: Pittsburgh played in Paris last week. Is their travel a factor?

Monken's refusal: "I'm done trying to figure out pluses and minuses to the short week, to the long week, to the international games." Then he told on himself with a story: "back in twenty twenty-three, we played a Monday night Christmas out in San Francisco... and we had to play the Dolphins coming back on a short week. We didn't get back to like six or seven in the morning. And I'll be darned if we didn't play our rear ends off the next Sunday."

Our ruling: the story checks out exactly. Monken was Baltimore's offensive coordinator. The Ravens beat the forty-niners thirty-three to nineteen on Christmas Day, Monday, December twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-three, in San Francisco, and the following Sunday, December thirty-first, they beat Miami fifty-six to nineteen. A coast-to-coast Monday night followed by a short week, and they put up fifty-six points.

He is refusing an excuse that would have been available to him in reverse, and he is refusing it with evidence. That is the most credible kind of refusal.

Refusal three. Jared Verse's trash talk.

The story offered: Jared Verse called the Steelers dirty after the October first game and doubled down this week. How do you feel about what he is putting out there?

The background is real and it is loud. After that Thursday-night win Verse said: "I'm like, 'Y'all double-teaming. Y'all sliding. Y'all garbage.' I was talking crazy to them," said he has no respect for players who do extra things after the whistle, and added "I can't wait to see them again." Before kickoff he was caught on the broadcast yelling "I hate black and yellow, man. I hate it." He got face to face with guard Mason McCormick at the end and took an unnecessary-roughness penalty. This week he added: "I'm not worried about Cam. I'm not worried about their O-line. I'm not worried about nobody they got on their team."

Pittsburgh answered. Cameron Heyward said the comments "will be addressed." Tackle Troy Fautanu said: "I don't know what he's talking about honestly," and declined to engage.

Monken's refusal: "I really haven't seen any of it. I've got a lot of respect for the Steelers and the physical style of play that they play with. And I would hope that we're trending in that direction to be that kind of team, because I think that carries. I think physicality, mental stamina, and that toughness carries, especially as the weather turns. I don't speak for another man. He speaks for himself."

Our ruling: that is close to a perfect answer, and it contains a mild correction of his own player. He did not defend the trash talk, did not disown Verse personally, and used the question to say he wants his team to become more like the one being insulted. Note the honest problem underneath: Verse has not recorded a sack as a Brown, and he is saying he is not worried about anybody on Pittsburgh's roster. That is a lot of weight to put on an afternoon. If he backs it up, it is a great story. If he does not, he handed a hostile stadium a reason to enjoy itself.

Refusal four. The run defense panic.

The story offered: you got away with a poor run-defense performance. Is the silver lining that you had a week to fix it?

Monken's refusal of the premise, while accepting the fix: "We're always under construction, and when you're playing against the best in the world, you're going to have games where they move the football... Do you want that to be the norm? Of course not. But there are times where you can have your ace pitcher and you might have to win ten to eight. It just happens that day." And: "luckily, situational football-wise, we stopped him on the goal line. We stopped him, forced him one time to kick a field goal."

Our ruling: half right, and the half he skipped is the dangerous one. The goal-line stand was real, Pollard's apparent touchdown overturned by replay with his knee down at the one, Cam Ward sneaking for nothing, Adin Huntington's sack, and a pile of Browns stopping Pollard on fourth-and-goal. Situational football genuinely saved that game.

But "ten to eight" understates it. Cleveland had allowed one rushing touchdown in its first six games and then conceded three in one afternoon. That is not an off day inside a stable baseline; that is the baseline being questioned. And the opponent on Sunday is better equipped than Tennessee, which brings us to the question the video never mentions.

The question about Pittsburgh that matters most.

Buried early in the transcript is the sharpest question anybody asked: the first time you played Pittsburgh, they had Jaylen Warren, but Rico Dowdle did not play. What does he add?

Monken: "Versatility. Another really good football player. Done it for a number of years, run and pass, and they're utilizing both those guys. He's a powerful runner. I think they're doing a great job utilizing both of them."

That is the whole Sunday problem in one exchange. On October first Cleveland faced one back. Now it faces two: Warren, who ran eighteen times for one hundred one yards at five point six a carry in Paris and has four games this season with one hundred twenty-five or more scrimmage yards, tied with Kenneth Walker for the league lead, and Dowdle, who added nine carries for forty-one and six catches for forty-five in that same game. Mike McCarthy has called them "the one two punch I was referring to back in training camp."

A defense that just gave up three rushing touchdowns, facing a two-back rotation it has not seen, with its interior anchor back from a multi-week absence. That is the matchup, and it is the one we will be charting at Ktl Report from the first snap.

The Watson arc, in Monken's own sequence.

Asked about Watson coming off what he had called his most complete game, Monken laid out the season as he sees it, and it is a useful piece of bookkeeping:

"I saw it starting really with the first Pittsburgh game, where I thought he played well. Then we went up in the rain and we didn't play well. We didn't coach well. We didn't do things well enough to win. And then the first half against Baltimore, they're pretty good on defense and we just could never get anything going. Then the second half was pretty good. And then last game was probably his most complete game, and us complete. I mean us as a whole offense."

Four games, four verdicts, and the coach taking the Jets loss on himself and his staff first: "none of us were worth the darn." On the comparison question he was blunter still, "I don't know how you compare anything to the Jets game."

The final reports, both teams.

Cleveland: Tytus Howard questionable (concussion), Myles Harden questionable (hamstring), Daniel Thomas questionable (ankle). Mike Hall Junior carries no designation and will play.

Pittsburgh: Brandin Echols out (hamstring), Doneiko Slaughter out (hamstring), two cornerbacks gone, and Ben Skowronek questionable (calf). T.J. Watt and Cameron Heyward took Wednesday off for rest, not injury, which means they are being pointed at this game rather than away from it.

Two Steelers corners out is the most exploitable thing on either sheet, and it is the argument for Cleveland throwing early rather than waiting until it is behind.

The bottom line.

Fourteen minutes in Berea produced one piece of real news, one roster return, four refused narratives, and an admission.

The news is a single word: asked whether Tytus Howard can still play without having practiced, Monken said yes, and the final report lists him questionable with his coach saying he is on track to potentially clear. The return is Mike Hall Junior, with no designation, whose job is exactly the thing that broke last week.

The four refusals tell you more about the coach than any of it. He would not credit one player for the protection, would not use Pittsburgh's trip to Paris, would not join his own defensive end's trash talk, and would not panic about a run defense that got shredded, while accepting it needs fixing.

And the admission is the headline, rendered honestly: he knows who his first two quarterbacks are, and he has not yet decided on the third. Four passers, two spots settled, one laugh, and a game in a stadium where Cleveland has not won a regular-season game since two thousand three.
