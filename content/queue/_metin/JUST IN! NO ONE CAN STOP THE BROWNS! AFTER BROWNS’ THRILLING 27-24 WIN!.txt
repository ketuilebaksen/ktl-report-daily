The pattern. A lead, then a fourth quarter.

Tampa: Cleveland led, Tampa came back, and it took a two-hour lightning delay and a defensive stand to finish.
 
Carolina: Cleveland led thirteen to three, Carolina scored fifteen unanswered and went ahead, and it took a go-ahead drive in the last two minutes.
 
Pittsburgh: Cleveland led twenty-one to seven, and Rodgers tied it with one thirty-six left.
 
Three times now, this team has built a lead and then had to win the game twice. And the mechanism is the same every week, which is what makes it a pattern rather than three coincidences: the offense stops producing first downs, the defense plays too many snaps, and the opponent's best drives come late against a tired front.
 
The fix is not on defense. Sustained drives are how you protect a lead. If Cleveland converts third downs in the third quarter, Rodgers never gets the ball back with a chance to tie.
 
But notice the other half, and it's real: they have now won all three of those games. A team that wins the close ones in October is a team that believes it can, and that belief is not a small asset in a division where every game is a knife fight.
