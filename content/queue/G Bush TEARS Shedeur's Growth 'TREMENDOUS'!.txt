First, the title. And the misattribution at the heart of it.

The title is "G Bush Tears Shedeur's Growth 'Tremendous'!"

Two problems, and the second one is the serious one.

First, "tears." Nobody tore anything or anyone apart in this video. The radio host in question criticised media coverage, specifically, the amount of airtime being given to a college quarterback. He did not attack Shedeur Sanders, Deshaun Watson, Todd Monken, the front office or any player. He went after the business of talking about football. "Tears" belongs to a different video.

Second, and this is the one that matters: the word "tremendous" is in the transcript, and the host is not the person who said it.

Listen to the cold open:

"I just think the progress that your backup quarterback has made has been tremendous. I thought he did a great job."

That is a clip. That is the guest, a coach the show brought on to grade the film. The host's own line, seconds later, is the giveaway: "One word kept coming up. Tremendous." He is quoting somebody else and saying so.

So a title that reads "G Bush Tears Shedeur's Growth 'Tremendous'" has taken a coach's evaluation and hung it on a radio host's name, and then added a verb nobody earned.

Name one. Jordan Reid, the man who actually made the pick.

"I have them taking LaNorris Sellers, the quarterback out of South Carolina... That is an accurate summary of the argument and it is the single most important sentence in this video. A projection about playing time is not an evaluation of a player. They are different claims and only one of them is checkable.

And the mechanics of the pick are worth knowing: Reid has Sellers twenty-fifth on his own big board and slid him to Cleveland at seventeen, "with the Rams pick from the Myles Garrett trade."
