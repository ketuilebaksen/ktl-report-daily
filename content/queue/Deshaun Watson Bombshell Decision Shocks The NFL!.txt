The sixth story. The goal-line menu.

The best football mechanics in the whole transcript, and it answers a question fans ask every week: who decides the play at the goal line?

"throughout the week and before the game, we talk about certain things, but ultimately it's the coaching staff, you know, his call and what he feel like is best in that situation. Read that carefully, because it describes a specific and slightly unusual arrangement.

"He give us a couple plays where we can choose from."

That is a menu. At the goal line, Monken is not always sending in one play, he is sending in options and letting the quarterback pick based on what he sees. That is a real delegation of authority, and it is a notable amount of trust to hand a quarterback in a first-year head coach's first season.

And Watson is careful to say where the credit goes: "ultimately it's the coaching staff... his call." Twice. The menu is the coach's. The pick is the quarterback's.

The reporter also references two specific goal-line calls, a counter to a player the transcript renders as "KC," and a design that kept the ball with Watson himself. A quarterback keeper at the goal line is a call you only make if you trust the quarterback's legs and his decision-making, and for a man described as coming back from twenty-two months, being given the ball on the ground inside the five is its own kind of statement from a coaching staff.
