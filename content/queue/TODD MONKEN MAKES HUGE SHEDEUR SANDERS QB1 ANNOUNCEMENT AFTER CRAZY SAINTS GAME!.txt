The timestamp. This press conference was not recorded after the Saints game, and we can prove it with five lines from inside the tape.

First, the title, and this is the most serious problem we have found in a month.

The title is "Todd Monken Makes Huge Shedeur Sanders Q B one Announcement After Crazy Saints Game!"

There is no Q B one announcement in this footage. And the footage was not recorded after the Saints game.

That second part is the one that matters, so we are not going to assert it and move on. We are going to prove it from inside the tape itself, using only what Monken says, with no outside sources required. Five pieces of evidence, each one sufficient on its own.

This is a different kind of error from the ones we normally correct here at Ktl Report. A mangled name is carelessness. An inflated headline is marketing. Presenting a press conference from before a game as a reaction to that game is something else, and the reason we are spending a whole article on it is that it is the easiest mistake in the world to make with a folder full of clips, and the hardest one for a viewer to catch.

So let us catch it, and then let us tell you what is actually in this tape, because it is excellent, and it deserves better than a false frame.

The forensic dating. Five lines that timestamp the tape.

Exhibit one. Shedeur will take the reps today with the ones.

He says it twice:

"So he is in the protocol and Shedeur will take the reps today with the ones."
"But we'll let it play out. But Shedeur again, he'll take the reps today with the ones."

That sentence can only exist on a practice day. You do not tell a room that your backup is taking first-team reps "today" after a game has been played. It is a Wednesday sentence.

Exhibit two. I don't know how Deshaun will feel tomorrow.

"I don't know how tomorrow, Deshaun will feel tomorrow. I'm excited for today. I'm excited for us to go out there today. We'll be indoor because of the rain."

Indoor because of the rain. That is a man describing a practice field being swapped for a fieldhouse. And then, immediately after: "Hell, we're playing indoors, so that happens to work out", the game is in the future tense. Cleveland played New Orleans in a dome. He is saying the weather forced them inside and that this is convenient because the upcoming game will also be indoors.

You cannot say "we're playing indoors, so that happens to work out" after you have already played.

Exhibit three. Came in Tuesday and reported it.

"Credit to Deshaun, after the game felt fine, Monday night started feeling a little bit of a headache, and came in Tuesday and reported it. So, credit to him... so he is in the protocol."

This is the protocol being announced, not recalled. Watson took the hit on Sunday, felt it Monday night, reported it Tuesday morning, and the club announced the protocol on Wednesday. Monken is delivering that news here, in sequence, for the first time.

Exhibit four. The good news is we're five and three.

"The good news is we're five and three and we have every opportunity to flip that script."

And moments later:

"We have nine games left, I think. Is that right? Nine. Nine games. Minimum nine games left."

Cleveland went into the Saints game at five and three and came out of it at five and four. A head coach saying "we're five and three" with "nine games left" is a coach who has not yet played game ten. This single line dates the tape on its own.

Exhibit five. The first quarter number is the eight game number.

Q: "I know you guys are getting outscored like sixty-five to thirteen in the first quarter..."

That figure is the eight-game total. It was the number all week before New Orleans. A reporter quoting it as current is a reporter standing in the building before the ninth game.

Verdict on the timestamp: this is the Wednesday press conference from the week of the Saints game. Five independent pieces of internal evidence, all pointing the same way, none of which require you to look anything up. If you are going to use this footage, say so on screen: "earlier this week" or "before the game." It costs you four words and it is the difference between a highlight reel and a fabrication.

And there is no Q B one announcement in here either.

Separate from the dating problem, the other half of the title fails too.

Asked whether Watson was out, Monken said: "I wouldn't say that yet."

Asked whether he felt Sanders was going to start: "We'll let it play out."

Asked whether he would be uncomfortable playing Watson off a week without practice, he refused even that: "I don't know that. I'm not saying I won't. I mean, there's a long time between now and then."

Three chances to make an announcement. Three declines. The word "Q B one" appears nowhere. What appears instead is a coach assigning Wednesday's repetitions and saying the week would decide it.

And here is the detail that makes the "announcement" framing collapse completely: he even floated the possibility of playing Watson anyway, and reached for a comparison to another team to make the point, "look where the Ravens are at", before adding the honest caveat: "It's not ideal, right? It's not ideal for any player to go through a week and not practice and then play. We know that. But every situation is different."

That is a man keeping a door open, not closing one.

Now the good news. What is actually in this tape is better than the title.

Here is the frustrating part. This is one of the most informative press conferences Monken has given all season, and it has been wasted on a false frame. Five things in it are genuinely worth a video of their own.

One. He explained exactly why a quarterback's concussion return is harder than anyone else's.

This is the answer nobody has reported and it is the clearest explanation of the whole week:

Q: "When you say Deshaun's return is more difficult for a quarterback, do you mean because of the non-contact aspect?"
MONKEN: "I just mean in terms of the mental aspect of it. Just the position itself, I think, is much more difficult. If you're not able to take the team reps and the throwing reps, how do you get the timing with your receivers? I'm just saying it's very difficult."

That is the whole thing in two sentences. A returning lineman can clear a protocol and play on instinct and technique. A quarterback cannot, because his job is timing, and timing is a shared skill that exists between him and four or five other men, and it decays when you do not practise it.

And the comparison he drew is the useful part. He contrasted it directly with Tytus Howard, who had cleared the protocol the previous week: "unlike where Tytus could come back Saturday for a walkthrough." A right tackle can walk through it on a Saturday. A quarterback cannot throw timing into existence on a Saturday.

This single answer explains everything that followed on Sunday, why Watson, even once cleared, was inactive, and why Monken later answered a reporter's "is the reason just that he didn't practise?" with one word: "Exactly."

Two. He commended the NFL for taking the decision out of his own hands.

Asked how involved Watson could be while in the protocol, Monken said he could be in meetings and film work, that "he felt a lot better today," and then volunteered something a head coach does not have to say:

"That's part of what the NFL has put in place to protect players from themselves. And I think it's for the protection of the players, whether that's during the game, whether that's after the game, to take it really out of the players' and the coaches' hands, and I commend the NFL for that. Because ultimately what we really want to see from our players is play again, right? And be healthy, not only each week, but when their career is over."

"To protect players from themselves" and "when their career is over." A coach under pressure to win nine remaining games said the quiet part out loud: he is glad he is not allowed to make this call.

That is the headline nobody wrote this week, and it is a better one than anything about a depth chart.

Three. He credited Watson properly, and in detail.

Before any of the quarterback talk, he laid out the sequence and put the credit where it belonged: Watson felt fine after the game, developed a headache Monday night, came in Tuesday and reported it.

"Credit to him for someone who really loves playing the game."

A starting quarterback in the final year of his contract, on a five-and-three team, raised his hand about symptoms nobody could see. Monken said credit twice in one answer. Whatever anybody thinks about the rest of Watson's season, that is the correct thing to lead with and it took a week for most people to notice.

Four. He described how you accelerate a backup, step by step.

Asked how you make up for a backup's missing repetitions:

"He's going to get all the walkthrough reps, all the team reps. We've had our Hungry Dogs group that gets reps after practice with some of the young guys, that's a limited amount. He's been in all the meetings previously. He's seen all the game films. But we're going to have to get some extra walks in, some extra meetings in with him as we proceed. But he's been working diligently each week, with the expectation there's a chance he could play, which was true in any given game."

So the "Hungry Dogs group" is finally explained, and we can put it in the record properly: it is a post-practice reps group for young players, and Monken himself describes the volume as "a limited amount." We flagged this as unverified three weeks ago when a video mentioned it without explanation. Here is the explanation, from the only person qualified to give it.

Five. He laid out the slow-start diagnosis himself, category by category.

This is the most quotable football answer in the tape and nobody used it. Asked about being outscored sixty-five to thirteen in first quarters:

"A couple of them we've turned it over. The first game, and then this last game we turned it over, right? We didn't even give ourselves a chance. There's been others where we've gotten penalties that have knocked us back. There's others where we've had lost-yardage plays. I'm talking just offensively. And then defensively, obviously, your inability, at least initially, to start the game, to stop the run, right? So you're not putting it behind the chains."

Four named causes, three offensive and one defensive: turnovers, penalties, lost-yardage plays, and failing to stop the run early. That is a coach giving you his own diagnosis in public, unprompted, with no deflection.

And then the sentence that is the actual team philosophy:

"Our formula cannot be turning the ball over. Let's just start with that. We're not that type of team to be able to turn the ball over and expect to win. We got away with it two weeks ago. We didn't this past week."

"We got away with it two weeks ago. We didn't this past week." That is a head coach refusing to let a win cover up a problem, which is the rarest thing in this sport.

So what the tape actually predicted, graded against Sunday.

Here is the useful thing about discovering that footage predates a game: you can grade it. Monken made four implicit predictions in this press conference. Four days later we got the results.

Prediction one: a quarterback cannot get his timing back without team reps. becomes Confirmed. Watson was cleared on Saturday and made inactive anyway. The reason given afterwards was one word.

Prediction two: Sanders' summer connections with the starters would carry over. Asked how quickly the chemistry could come back after weeks off, he said simply: "Well, we're about to see." becomes Confirmed, emphatically. The two receivers he had thrown to all camp produced ninety-eight and eighty-five yards, and the rookie first-round pick's sixty-four-yard touchdown was the longest play of the game.

Prediction three: "our formula cannot be turning the ball over." becomes Confirmed as a standard, and met by the quarterback. Sanders threw thirty-one passes and no interceptions. The formula was followed at the position where it was most in doubt.

Prediction four: the first quarter was the thing to fix. becomes Failed. Cleveland committed six penalties in the opening quarter and was level at three apiece at the break. The category changed, penalties rather than turnovers, but the quarter was lost again.

Three of four confirmed. Which is to say: the press conference being sold as a reaction to the game was actually a reasonably accurate forecast of it, and here at Ktl Report we would rather hand you an accurate forecast four days early than a fake reaction four days late.

And the one moment of real joy, which is worth keeping.

Asked how a week of first-team practice would help a quarterback who had been denied it:

"Man, I can't wait. This is so cool. I mean, that's an opportunity. If it goes that way, then he gets a great opportunity to go play, and for us to see him go play... I can't wait to see him let it rip today and see where we're at. I mean, that's what you do. That's why you build depth. That's why he got a ton of reps in fall camp. That's really the benefit of all this, when we opened it up as a competition, he got a ton of those reps."

"This is so cool." A head coach of a five-and-three team, four days out from a road game, with his starting quarterback in a concussion protocol, said "this is so cool" about watching his young backup practise.

That is the clip. It is just not from after the game.

The names and numbers to fix.

"Shadur" and "Shador" are Shedeur Sanders.
"Deshawn" is Deshaun Watson.
"Titus" is Tytus Howard, and the comparison matters, so say the name right: he is the right tackle who cleared a concussion protocol the previous week and returned via a Saturday walkthrough.
"non-cont" is non-contact, the reporter's question is about whether the difficulty is the no-contact restriction.
"coach Pakian and Shawn" are two members of the coaching staff whose names the transcript garbles. Look them up and credit them properly, or leave the names out, we are not going to guess at a real person's name and neither should you.
"Ton" is Todd, a reporter beginning a question.
"sixty-five to thirteen" is sixty-five to thirteen, and it is the eight-game first-quarter figure, not a current one.
"five and three" is correct as of the recording, Cleveland is now five and four. If you use this clip, that mismatch is exactly what will give the game away to viewers.

What we could not verify.

The names of the two assistants referenced as being on the sideline with Sanders during games.
The precise date and time of this press conference. The internal evidence places it on the Wednesday of the Saints week with no realistic alternative, but we are dating it by inference and we are saying so.
Whether Watson was formally ruled out at any point, as opposed to being cleared and then made inactive.
Cleveland's sacks allowed and fumble count from the Saints game, still open from our own grading list.
The size and schedule of the "Hungry Dogs group" beyond Monken's own description of it as post-practice reps for young players in "a limited amount."

What we are not carrying.

We are not carrying "Q B one announcement." He said "I wouldn't say that yet," "we'll let it play out," and "I'm not saying I won't" about playing Watson. Three declines in one session.

And above all, we are not carrying this as post-game footage. Five lines inside the tape date it to the week before. Anyone using it owes viewers four words on screen.

We are not carrying any speculation about Deshaun Watson's head, his future or his contract. He reported symptoms honestly and his coach commended him for it twice.

We are not carrying "Monken benched Watson." A quarterback who takes no team repetitions cannot get his timing back, and Monken explained why in detail before anybody had asked the leading version of the question.

We are not carrying a diagnosis of the first quarter beyond the four causes Monken named himself.

The bottom line.

This press conference was not recorded after the Saints game, and you do not have to take our word for it. Monken says "Shedeur will take the reps today with the ones", twice. He says "we'll be indoor because of the rain" and then, of the game, "we're playing indoors, so that happens to work out", future tense. He announces that Watson felt a headache Monday night and "came in Tuesday and reported it." He says "the good news is we're five and three" and "nine games left." And a reporter quotes the first-quarter deficit as sixty-five to thirteen, which was the eight-game figure.

Five tells, all pointing to the Wednesday before the game. There is no Q B one announcement in it, because four days before kickoff there was nothing to announce, he said "I wouldn't say that yet," "we'll let it play out," and, about the possibility of playing Watson anyway, "I'm not saying I won't."

Which is a shame, because the real tape is better than the invented one. In it, a head coach explains why a quarterback cannot return from a protocol the way a right tackle can, "if you're not able to take the team reps and the throwing reps, how do you get the timing with your receivers?", and in doing so pre-answers the entire controversy of the following week. He commends the NFL for taking the decision out of his hands, says the aim is for players to be healthy "when their career is over," and credits his injured starter twice for reporting symptoms nobody else could see. He names the four causes of the worst first quarter in football, turnovers, penalties, lost-yardage plays and failing to stop the run early, and then refuses to let a victory hide the problem: "We got away with it two weeks ago. We didn't this past week."

And he says, about watching a young quarterback finally get a week of first-team repetitions: "Man, I can't wait. This is so cool."

Here at Ktl Report we audit the claim, not the channel, and the claim in this title is false twice over. But the footage underneath it is a four-day-old forecast that turned out to be three-quarters right, a quarterback's timing does not survive a week off, the summer chemistry did carry over, the ball did not get turned over, and the first quarter was lost anyway.
